# Aletheia

Aletheia is a research program on a **deterministic, symbolic "epistemic kernel"** for mathematics and physics.

**The idea in one paragraph.** Sometimes known objects cannot satisfy a law. Examples are x² = −1 over the rationals, or anticommuting quantities in a commutative setting. We call this a *deficit*. In that case the kernel should construct the **minimal new kind of object** that satisfies the law, the "freest algebra" or *mold* of the law. If something already known suffices, it should say so instead. While doing this it must never silently drop a role the law gives to a quantity, and never claim more than the givens decide: when the evidence does not settle a question, it lists the alternatives and the observation that would decide between them.

> **Status: early research, not a product.** Parts of the decision machinery have passed pre-registered blind tests on small formal worlds. There is **no evidence yet** of real scientific discovery, of performance on real (noisy) data, or of any advantage over large language models. See the results below, including a hypothesis of ours that failed.

## Results so far
| Study | What was tested | Result |
|---|---|---|
| [Blind test 1](studies/aletheia_blind_test_1/README.md) (2026-09-29) | Choosing the right kind and algebraic family for a law's unknowns, without "silent collapse". Classifying a filled law with its identity card. | **PASS.** 28/28 against the best baseline's 16/28 (McNemar p = 0.00024). Identity card: balanced accuracy 0.905 against 0.667. |
| [Blind test 2](studies/aletheia_blind_test_2/README.md) (2026-09-30) | Deciding when the givens decide, and forking with checkable decisive observations when they do not. Grading zeros without "false death". | **PASS.** 30/30, including 10/10 forks with certificates. 18/18, with 0 false deaths. |
| [LLM comparison 1](studies/aletheia_llm_compare_1/REPORT.md) (2026-10-01) | Do frontier LLMs execute the same rules as well, when given them in plain text? | **Our hypothesis was refuted.** DeepSeek, Gemini and Qwen scored 30/30 and 18/18. Claude did the same after a declared correction of one corrupted transcript. The kernel's edge is **not** rule execution. |

**What these results show**
- The decision rules can be computed exactly on unseen formal worlds, and every verdict comes with a machine-checkable certificate.
- The rules are clear enough that four families of LLMs execute them perfectly when given them.

**What they do not show**
- Real discovery.
- Behaviour on noisy data.
- Whether LLMs follow this discipline when they are *not* given the rules. This is untested.
- Any accuracy advantage of the kernel over LLMs.

**Shared limitations**
- The worlds are small and formal, and they were generated by a Claude-family model.
- "Correct" is defined by our own rules.
- The 95% confidence lower bounds for the perfect scores are about 81-88%.

## How the studies were run
Every study is pre-registered:
1. The procedure and scorer are sealed by SHA-256 before any test world exists.
2. Worlds and answer keys come from a separate generator session.
3. The key hash is published before the procedure runs, and the outputs hash is published before the key is opened.
4. Reports are published verbatim.
5. Post-hoc audits are kept separate from outcomes.

All three studies reproduce **bit for bit** from this repository. See [studies/README.md](studies/README.md) for the hashes and the commands.
