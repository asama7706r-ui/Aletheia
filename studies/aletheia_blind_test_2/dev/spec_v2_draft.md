# Aletheia blind test 2 - DRAFT specification (NOT sealed)

Status: draft for review by the project owner (2026-09-30). Nothing here is sealed.
After review: implement procedure_v2.py and score_v2.py on dev cases only, run the regression checks (Section 9), then seal by publishing hashes before any test world exists. Same roles and order as test 1 (protocol_v1.md, Section 0).

## 0. What changed since test 1, and why
- Test 1 PASSED. Its audit showed that role rule v1 ("every quantity a law mentions must stay nonzero") avoids silent collapse when the laws state the role. It also found the untested opposite error: a law may mention a quantity that is zero in the truth (the over-loose trap). See Q9 page, Section 7.
- Decisions of 2026-09-30 (Q9 page, Section 7.9):
  - D1: decide when the givens decide; fork with a decisive observation when they do not. No weights, no probabilities, no partial credit.
  - D2: observations are the kernel's external input.
  - D3: exact rationals throughout. An observation is an exact interval set by the observer's resolution, and it never establishes an exact zero.
  - D4: the "nebulium check" is test 3, not this test.
- Principle of conservatism (the kernel crawls up from the known): known rules are kept unless a given forces relaxing them, and the kernel never raises a possibility that no given raises.

## 1. Claims under test
- Part A (primary). Given laws plus observations of nonzero quantities, the procedure:
  - decides exactly when the givens determine the answer;
  - otherwise returns the correct fork, with valid decisive observations and certificates.
- Part B (secondary). Given a law with a candidate term, declared constants (bridges), declared exact symmetries, parameter values, and exact observations with resolutions, the procedure classifies the term into the grades of zero (plus ACTIVE and CONFLICT) without "false death".
- Not tested:
  - the nebulium check and the search for return conditions (test 3);
  - where roles come from when nothing states them (9b);
  - auto-filling the identity card (9c);
  - non-associative structures;
  - speed.

## 2. Part A definitions
- Generators, grades, variables, parameters and laws are as in protocol v1, Sections 2-3.
- Observations (demands) have the form {"expr": E, "status": "nonzero"}.
  - Part A has no zero-observations. An algebra element has no magnitude, so "below resolution" is undefined for it. Zero-at-resolution is Part B's subject.
- Mentions are the nodes that contain a generator in the parse tree of each side of each law.
  - N-ary sums and products are flattened, so (a*b)*c is the product a*b*c. Its mentions are a, b, c and a*b*c, but NOT a*b.
  - a - b is a + (-1)*b.
  - A power a**3 mentions a and a**3. (a*b)**2 mentions a*b.
  - A mention is live if it is nonzero in the assoc mold.
- Levels, in preference order: SUBSUMED (rational values) < comm < graded < assoc. graded is used only if some generator is odd.
- An admissible algebra is a nonzero unital associative Q-algebra generated by the generators that satisfies the laws for all variable values, with every demand nonzero.
- Level L is viable iff an admissible algebra exists at L.
  - SUBSUMED: some rational solution keeps every demand nonzero.
  - Other levels: L's mold is nontrivial and every demand is alive in it. The mold is itself admissible.
- F_occ is the first viable level.
- F_role is the first viable level that also keeps every live mention nonzero. For SUBSUMED, this means some viable rational solution keeps all live mentions nonzero.
  - F_role exists whenever any level is viable, because assoc keeps every live mention by definition.
- Correct answer:
  - CONTRADICTION if no level is viable: 1 = 0 in the assoc mold, or a demand is zero there.
  - DETERMINED(F) if F_occ = F_role = F. For SUBSUMED, the values are the viable solutions that keep every live mention nonzero.
  - FORK otherwise.
    - The branches are the viable levels from F_occ up to F_role, in preference order.
    - A non-last branch B_i holds iff the live mentions killed at B_i are zero.
    - The last branch, F_role, holds iff those mentions are nonzero.
    - Each non-last branch has a decisive set Q_i: observing every element of Q_i nonzero excludes B_i and stays compatible with F_role.
    - One-way resolution (consequence of D3): an observation can exclude the stricter branch, but "below resolution" can never confirm it. In the kernel, a stricter branch that is never excluded becomes the working answer, and the quantity is recorded as a bounded lurking relation. It is never recorded as dead.
  - UNDERDETERMINED if the answer (label and levels) changes with a parameter value on the grid, as in v1.
- Truth: the hidden witness T must be consistent with the givens, and T's level must be F (DETERMINED) or one of the branches (FORK).
  - A world whose truth is looser than F_role is INVALID, because no given raises that possibility (conservatism).

## 3. Part A procedure (outline)
1. Parse the world.
2. Build A, the truncated assoc mold, as in v1. If A is trivial, or a demand is dead in A, answer CONTRADICTION.
3. Compute the live mentions, then level viability, F_occ, F_role and the branches.
4. Choose decisive sets:
   - Noncommutative or comm branch: Q_i = {the first live mention killed at B_i}, in a fixed order. It is zero in B_i's mold and alive in F_role's mold.
   - SUBSUMED branch: a small set of killed live mentions such that every viable rational solution kills at least one (greedy hitting set), all alive in F_role's mold.
5. Attach certificates:
   - At a graded or assoc branch, for each q in Q_i: an ideal-membership certificate q = sum of c * u * r * v, where u and v are words and r is a law relation or a family rule. It is extracted from the echelon reduction.
   - At a comm branch: the scorer verifies membership with its own sympy Groebner computation.
6. Parameters: as in v1 (grid sampling; differing signatures give UNDERDETERMINED).
7. Output one of:
   - {"label": "DETERMINED", "level": L, "values": [...]}
   - {"label": "FORK", "branches": [{"level": L, "killed": [...]}, ...], "decisive": [[...], ...], "certificates": [...]}
   - {"label": "CONTRADICTION"}
   - {"label": "UNDERDETERMINED", "variants": [...]}

## 4. Part A world and key formats
- World: the v1 format plus "observations": [{"expr": "...", "status": "nonzero"}].
- Key entry:
  - "id";
  - "answer": DETERMINED | FORK | CONTRADICTION | UNDERDETERMINED;
  - "level" and "values" (DETERMINED);
  - "branches": [{"level", "killed": [mentions], "killed_certificates", "witness"}] (FORK);
  - "truth_branch" and "truth_witness";
  - "exclusions": for each non-viable level below F_occ, a certificate that 1 or some demand lies in that level's ideal;
  - "true_parameter_values", "category", "notes".
- Every branch has a witness: rational matrices that satisfy the laws, keep every demand nonzero, have the branch level as their preference-first family, and make the branch's killed mentions vanish.
- The scorer does NOT import the procedure. It checks:
  - witnesses by matrix arithmetic;
  - noncommutative certificates by polynomial expansion;
  - commutative membership and rational solutions with its own sympy code.
- Certificate format (to be fixed before sealing): a list of [coef, left_word, relation_ref, right_word]. relation_ref points to a law's variable-monomial coefficient relation or to a family rule.

## 5. Part A composition (30 worlds)
- DETERMINED: 13.
  - 6 resolved by demands against a killed mention: comm 2, graded 2, assoc 2.
  - 3 conservative, where no given raises a looser possibility.
  - 4 SUBSUMED, at least one with a demand that excludes a killing solution.
- FORK: 10.
  - 5 with the truth in the first branch (over-loose traps).
  - 5 with the truth in the last branch (silent-collapse traps).
  - At least 2 with three branches, and at least 2 with a SUBSUMED branch.
- CONTRADICTION: 4. Two are inconsistent laws; two are demands the laws kill.
- UNDERDETERMINED: 3. At least one has a parameter that switches between DETERMINED and FORK.
- Other constraints:
  - At least a third of the non-contradiction worlds lie outside the familiar list of protocol v1, Section 5.
  - No test-1 world and no dev world is reproduced.

## 6. Part A baselines, metrics and criteria
- Baselines:
  - V1D: the v1 rule with demands added, answering DETERMINED(F_role).
  - OCC: DETERMINED(F_occ).
  - FORKALL: whenever F_occ != assoc, answer FORK[F_occ, assoc], with decisive = the first live mention killed at F_occ.
- Correctness:
  - The label must match.
  - DETERMINED also needs the level, and for SUBSUMED the listed values must contain the true values.
  - FORK also needs the branch level sequence to be equal, and every decisive set to be valid. Valid means its certificate verifies (or, at a SUBSUMED branch, each viable solution kills an element of it) and every element is nonzero on the last branch's witness.
  - UNDERDETERMINED needs the label only.
- Metrics:
  - accuracy;
  - accuracy on FORK worlds;
  - over-claim rate: DETERMINED answered on FORK worlds;
  - over-fork rate: FORK answered on DETERMINED worlds;
  - silent collapse and over-loose on DETERMINED worlds;
  - contradictions accepted.
- Criteria:
  - A1: accuracy >= 0.70.
  - A2: accuracy on FORK worlds >= 0.70.
  - A3: over-fork rate <= 0.15.
  - A4 (non-inferiority): on DETERMINED worlds, at most 1 world worse than the better of V1D and OCC.
  - A5: at most 1 contradiction accepted.
  - Reported but not a criterion: McNemar against the best baseline. It is partly definitional, because V1D and OCC cannot fork.
- Outcome: PASS if A1-A5 all hold; PARTIAL if A1 and A3 hold; FAIL otherwise; VOID if more than 20% of worlds are excluded.

## 7. Part B (secondary): grades of zero with exact observations
World format:
```
{"id": "B-01", "experiment": "B",
 "quantities": {"y": {...dims}, "x": {...dims}},
 "constants": {"c": {"value": "3", "dims": {...}}},
 "parameters": {"k": "1"},
 "law": "y = 2*x",
 "term": {"expr": "lam*x**3", "coefficient": "lam"},
 "coefficient_form": "free | constants",
 "symmetries": [{"name": "...", "map": {...}}],
 "observations": [{"inputs": {...}, "output": "...", "resolution": "..."}]}
```
Classes, applied in this order:
1. TYPE_IMPOSSIBLE applies only when coefficient_form is "constants", meaning the coefficient is a dimensionless number times a product of rational powers of the declared constants.
   - Let D* = dim(left side) - dim(term without its coefficient).
   - The term is impossible iff no product of rational powers of the declared constants has dimension D*. This is an exact linear system over Q.
   - A declared constant that solves it is a bridge.
2. SYMMETRY_ZERO: some declared symmetry maps the full law (base law plus term) to itself only if the coefficient is 0. This is checked symbolically and exactly.
3. INCIDENTAL: the coefficient is an expression in the parameters that is 0 at the given values but not identically 0.
4. Each observation gives an exact interval for coefficient * g(inputs). Intersecting them gives S, the feasible set of the coefficient, with exact rational endpoints.
   - An observation with g = 0 that the base law misses by more than the resolution gives CONFLICT.
   - If rules 1-3 give an exact-zero reason: the answer is CONFLICT if 0 is not in S (the term is detected), otherwise that class.
   - Otherwise the answer is:
     - CONFLICT if S is empty;
     - ACTIVE if 0 is not in S;
     - UNCONSTRAINED if no observation constrains the coefficient;
     - otherwise BOUNDED(B), where B = max |s| over S, exact.
- Truth: the generator's hidden coefficient value and hidden reason must be consistent with the key's class.
- Baselines:
  - THRESH: ZERO if 0 is in S, otherwise ACTIVE. It ignores symmetry, type and parameters. This is the "false death" baseline.
  - MENTION: ACTIVE unless dimensionally impossible.
  - DIMS: TYPE_IMPOSSIBLE or BOUNDED only.
- Composition (18 worlds):
  - ACTIVE 3, BOUNDED 3, UNCONSTRAINED 1, SYMMETRY_ZERO 3, TYPE_IMPOSSIBLE 3, INCIDENTAL 2, CONFLICT 3.
  - At least 2 worlds of other classes have a bridge-rescued term, where a declared constant makes the term possible.
  - At least 1 CONFLICT is a symmetry-violating detection, parity-violation style.
- Correctness: the class must match. BOUNDED also needs B exactly, and ACTIVE also needs S exactly.
- Criteria:
  - B1: accuracy >= 0.80.
  - B2: false-death rate <= 1/6. False death means answering an exact-zero class (TYPE_IMPOSSIBLE, SYMMETRY_ZERO, INCIDENTAL, ZERO) on BOUNDED or UNCONSTRAINED worlds.
  - B3: at least 2/3 of CONFLICT worlds detected.
  - B4: balanced accuracy above every baseline.
- Honest note: Part B mostly tests exact, mechanical classification on unseen worlds, as E2 did in test 1. Its baselines lack the taxonomy by design.

## 10. Known limitations (to be declared at sealing)
- The generator and the procedure author are models of the same family. Option: have Gemini generate part of the worlds, with every key certificate-checked by the scorer.
- Part of the comparison is definitional: the baselines never fork. The informative parts are correct forks, no over-forking, and certificates on unseen worlds.
- Conservatism is a design choice. It is not tested here.
- The truncated word engine can miss collapses and errs toward "alive". Certificates catch false "dead" claims but not false "alive" claims.
- Part B is mechanical (see its note).
