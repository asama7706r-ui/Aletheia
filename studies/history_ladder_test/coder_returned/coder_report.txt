PLAIN-TEXT REPORT: STEPS 0, 1, AND 2
========================================================================

1. SUMMARY OF INCLUSION AND EXCLUSION (STEP 2)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Included (include: true) — 4 cases:
  - C1 (tessarines): Introduced by James Cockle in 1848–1849 (S01–S03;
    reintroduced as bicomplex numbers by Corrado Segre in 1892, S04, W01)
    to symbolize algebraic impossibility from congeneric surd equations
    (1 + sqrt(j) = 0 => j^2 = +1), isolate hyperbolic series, and bring
    3D space under commutative ordinary algebra.
  - C2 (multicomplex numbers): Introduced by Corrado Segre in 1892 (S04,
    W02; dated Sept. 1891) as s-complex numbers (2^s real dimensions from
    s commuting imaginary units squaring to -1) to remove geometric
    exceptions and case distinctions in intersections of (s - 1)-hyper-
    algebraic varieties while preserving ordinary arithmetic laws.
  - C3 (hyperbolic quaternions): Introduced by Alexander Macfarlane in
    1891 (W03, S05) to provide a full vector product with a positive
    scalar part (i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = +1) in harmony with Gibbs-Heaviside
    vector analysis and to express trigonometry on equilateral
    hyperboloids.
  - C4 (split-biquaternions): Introduced by William Kingdon Clifford in
    1873 (Paper XX in S06/clifford_transcription.txt, W04; further
    developed in 1876 Papers *XLII-*XLIII) to represent screws, twists,
    and rigid-body force/velocity systems ("motors", whose sum depends on
    axis position) and their ratios q + omega*r (or xi*q + eta*r) in
    3D elliptic space where omega^2 = 1.

* Excluded (include: false) — 8 cases:
  - C5 (split-octonions, W00, W05)
  - C6 (complex octonions, W00, W06)
  - C7 (tensor product H x H, W00)
  - C8 (quaternionic matrix H x H x H, W00, W07)
  - C9 (trigintaduonions, W00, W08)
  - C10 (sexagintaquatronions, W00, W08)
  - C11 (centumduodetrigintanions, W00, W08)
  - C12 (ducentiquinquagintasexions, W00, W08)
  Reason for exclusion: In all 8 cases, only secondary overview files
  (W00, W05–W08) were provided. While those files define the basis and
  multiplication rules (allowing all 14 algebraic properties P1–P14 to be
  computed and verified by code), they do not identify who first
  constructed the specific number system, when it was first introduced,
  or what an identifiable author wanted before constructing it.

2. SUMMARY OF ALGEBRAIC PROPERTIES (P1–P14 COMPUTED BY CODE)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
* P1 (add. associativity), P2 (add. commutativity), P3 (add. identity 0),
  P4 (add. inverses), P7 (mult. identity 1 != 0), P8 (left distributivity),
  P9 (right distributivity), P10 (closure of multiplication), and
  P11 (0*a = a*0 = 0):
  - HOLD in all 12 cases (C1–C12).

* P12 (no nonzero zero divisors), P13 (multiplicative inverses for all
  nonzero elements), and P14 (compatible total order):
  - FAIL in all 12 cases (C1–C12).
  - Explicit zero-divisor counterexamples verified by code:
    C1: (1 + j)*(1 - j) = 0
    C2: (i - h)*(i + h) = 0  [and (1 + i*h)*(1 - i*h) = 0]
    C3: (1 + i)*(1 - i) = 0
    C4: (1 + omega)*(1 - omega) = 0  [or xi*eta = 0]
    C5: (1 + l)*(1 - l) = 0
    C6: (e_0 + i*e_1)*(e_0 - i*e_1) = 0
    C7: (1 + i_1*i_2)*(1 - i_1*i_2) = 0
    C8: (1 + i*I)*(1 - i*I) = 0  [or E_11 * E_22 = 0 in M_4(H)]
    C9–C12: (e_1 + e_10)*(e_4 - e_15) = 0

* P5 (associativity of multiplication) and P6 (commutativity):
  - C1 (tessarines) & C2 (multicomplex C_n): P5 HOLDS, P6 HOLDS.
  - C4 (split-biquaternions), C7 (H x H), & C8 (H x H x H): P5 HOLDS,
    P6 FAILS.
  - C5 (split-octonions) & C6 (complex octonions): P5 FAILS (e.g.
    (i*j)*l = -l*k != l*k = i*(j*l) in C5; (e_1*e_2)*e_4 = e_7 != -e_7 =
    e_1*(e_2*e_4) in C6), P6 FAILS. Weaker forms of P5 that HOLD:
    left-alternativity, right-alternativity, flexibility, and
    power-associativity.
  - C3 (hyperbolic quaternions, 4D system in W03): P5 FAILS ((i*j)*j = -i
    != i = i*(j*j)), P6 FAILS. Left-alternativity and right-alternativity
    also FAIL, whereas flexibility ((a*b)*a = a*(b*a)) and
    power-associativity ((a*a)*a = a*(a*a)) HOLD. (In Macfarlane's 1900
    modified 8D complexified algebra in S05 p. 170 with
    i_0*j_0 = sqrt(-1)*k_0, full associativity P5 HOLDS.)
  - C9 (32D), C10 (64D), C11 (128D), & C12 (256D): P5 FAILS
    ((e_1*e_2)*e_4 = e_7 != -e_7 = e_1*(e_2*e_4)), P6 FAILS.
    Left-alternativity and right-alternativity BOTH FAIL (for
    a = e_1 + e_10, b = e_4, (a*a)*b = -2*e_4 != -2*e_4 - 2*e_15 =
    a*(a*b)), whereas flexibility and power-associativity HOLD.

3. DOUBTS, AMBIGUITIES, AND LIMITATIONS IN THE PROVIDED SOURCES
------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. OCR Artifacts in Primary Text Files (S01–S05):
   - S01_Cockle_1848.txt, S02_Cockle_1849a.txt, S03_Cockle_1849b.txt,
     S04_Segre_1892.txt, and S05_Macfarlane_1900.txt are unedited OCR
     texts from 19th-century scans. Many inline equations are corrupted
     by OCR (for instance, in S02 p. 42, "(1+j)(1-j) = 1-1=0" appears as
     "(H-i)(l-i) = 1-1=0", and on p. 46 Greek letters alpha, beta, gamma
     appear as "a, /3, and y"; S01 and S02 also contain 0x0c form-feed
     characters). All quotes in cases_coded.json preserve the exact
     character sequences of the files as required, while mathematical
     meaning is cross-verified from the authors' verbal descriptions and
     secondary sources W01–W03.
2. Citation Mismatch in W04 for S06 ("page 395" vs Archive.org leaf n493):
   - W04_Split-biquaternion.txt (line 72) cites Clifford's Mathematical
     Papers "page 181 to 200" (Paper XX, PDF pp. 271–290) and "page 395"
     linked to Archive.org ".../page/n493/mode/2up". In S06, Archive.org
     leaf n493 is PDF page 494 (printed pp. 400–401, inside Paper *XLIII
     "On the Classification of Geometric Algebras", pp. 397–401), whereas
     printed p. 395 is PDF page 489 ("*Notes on Biquaternions", pp.
     395–396), which immediately follows Paper *XLII "[Further Note on
     Biquaternions]" (pp. 385–394, PDF pp. 479–488). To ensure complete
     coverage of every page referenced either by printed page number or
     Archive.org leaf number, clifford_transcription.txt transcribes
     Paper XX (PDF pp. 271–290) and the full contiguous range from PDF
     page 479 to PDF page 495 (printed pp. 385–401).
3. Two Distinct Systems Called "Biquaternions" in Clifford 1873 (C4):
   - In Paper XX (1873), Clifford first defines omega in flat/parabolic
     Euclidean 3-space where omega^2 = 0 (PDF pp. 276–278, now called
     dual quaternions) before moving to Section IV ("On the Geometry of
     Elliptic Space", PDF pp. 279–290) where omega^2 = 1 (split-
     biquaternions). Both choices and the elliptic definition omega^2 = 1
     are documented in C4.
4. Two Distinct Systems Called "Hyperbolic Quaternions" by Macfarlane (C3):
   - W03 defines hyperbolic quaternions as Macfarlane's 1891/1894 4D
     non-associative system (i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = +1, ij = k = -ji).
     However, the provided primary paper S05_Macfarlane_1900.txt is
     Macfarlane's 1900 modification in which he rejects the non-
     associative product (p. 169) and introduces sqrt(-1) into the cross
     products (i_0*j_0 = sqrt(-1)*k_0 = -j_0*i_0, p. 170) to regain
     associativity in an 8D complexified algebra. Both stages are
     documented in C3.
5. Source Coverage of W08 for C11 and C12:
   - 01_CASES.md lists W00 and W08 as the sources for C11 (128D
     centumduodetrigintanions) and C12 (256D ducentiquinquagintasexions).
     While W00 names and tabulates both (lines 83, 103, 105),
     W08_Trigintaduonion.txt stops at the 64D sexagintaquatronions (lines
     27 and 1266) and does not mention C11 or C12 by name.
